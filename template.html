Worked out in your browser for Cape Town; no outside service. Times SAST.

Solunar majors are the two hours around the moon passing overhead and underfoot; minors are the hour around moonrise and moonset. Many anglers plan by them. The evidence that they matter is weak, so they are shown here and on each chart but do not move any score. Springs and neaps do matter at sea: they are judged from the moon and shown with the model's tidal range.

Species guide
How the rating works

Every hour at every spot gets a score out of 10 for each fish that lives there, and the spot's score is the best of them. A fish's score starts from how common it is at that spot and whether it is in season, then the conditions of the hour multiply in. One bad factor pulls the score down without zeroing it, because fish still get caught on bad days.

Sea and estuary spots

  • Tide stage and flow. From the model's sea level: where the water sits in the local range, whether it is pushing or dropping, and how fast it moves. Each spot has a preferred stage from local anglers and each fish has its own preference and need for moving water.
  • Springs or neaps. Judged from the moon (springs follow new and full moon by a day or two). Some fish favour the bigger water movement of springs, some the clearer water of neaps.
  • Light. Dawn and dusk are the feeding windows for most predators; overcast days stretch them. Each fish has its own weights for night, dawn, bright day, dull day and dusk.
  • Sea state. The open-sea wave height is cut by how sheltered the spot is. Galjoen and kob like some white water; reef fish and harbour fish like it calmer. Above a safe height for the stand (rocks, beach or harbour wall), the hour is marked dangerous and scored near zero.
  • Wind. Light tackle needs fishable wind: the score drops steadily above about 15 km/h. Onshore, offshore and cross are shown against the direction the spot faces.
  • Water temperature. The model's sea-surface temperature against each fish's range, with a penalty when it has crashed by more than 2 °C in a day (south-easter upwelling turns the Atlantic side off).
  • Pressure trend and cloud. A falling barometer before a front tends to switch fish on; a fast rise behind it slows them. Small weights.

Freshwater spots

  • Water temperature carries the most weight. Nobody measures it at these vleis and rivers, so it is estimated from the last three weeks of air temperature, smoothed for the size of the water (shallow vleis react in days, rivers faster, dams slower). It is an estimate and says so.
  • Light (bass and trout feed best at dawn and dusk and in overcast), wind (a light breeze helps bass; strong wind ruins casting), pressure trend and season (spawning and winter slow-downs).
  • The moon counts only as night light, and only a little.

Labels

Dead under 1.5, Slow under 3.5, Fair under 5.5, Good under 7.5, Very good under 9, Excellent above. A fish in its closed season is not rated. The best time to go is the best fishable hour: from first light to two hours after sunset, or every hour with Night fishing ticked.

What it cannot know

  • Ocean cells are several kilometres across, so the sea state and water temperature are for the nearby open water, not the gully you stand over.
  • Bait shoals, water clarity on the day, river levels, weed, crowds and boats are not in the numbers.
  • The fish weights come from angling writing and biology references and are a starting point. The catch log is how to tune them: log what you catch and the conditions are saved with it.
Safety
  • Freak waves on the rocks. Most Cape angling deaths are anglers washed off rocks. Never turn your back on the sea, watch ten minutes of sets before you settle, and stay well back on the big-swell hours this page marks.
  • Wear grippy soles on wet rock and weed; take a friend to the remote ledges (Rooikrans, Platboom, Gifkommetjie, Bontkop).
  • NSRI emergency: 087 094 9774, or 112 from a cellphone.
  • Personal safety. Several spots on this page carry warnings about muggings or car break-ins in the angling sources; they are listed on each spot. Fish them in company and in daylight.
  • Water quality. After rain, the City often warns against contact with water at vleis, river mouths and beaches near stormwater outlets. Check the City of Cape Town's water-quality notices before eating fish from urban vleis.

Data: Open-Meteo marine and weather forecasts (open-meteo.com, free, non-commercial). Sea state, sea level and sea temperature come from the nearest ocean model cell; wind, cloud, rain and pressure from the nearest weather cell. The tide curve is the model's sea-level signal, good for timing, not a replacement for the SA Navy tide tables. Freshwater temperatures are estimates from air temperature.

Ratings are a model, not a fishing report. Nobody has checked the water. Know the rules before you keep a fish, and look after yourself on the rocks.
